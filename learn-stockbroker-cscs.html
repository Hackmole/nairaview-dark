Stockbrokers and CSCS accounts

In short

You cannot buy NGX shares directly — a licensed stockbroker places the order for you, and your shares are held electronically in your CSCS account. Opening one needs your BVN and a valid ID, and many brokers and apps now do it fully online.

What a stockbroker does

A stockbroker is your agent on the exchange: you say “buy 100 shares of GTCO,” they execute it and charge a small commission. In Nigeria, stockbrokers must be licensed by the SEC — check before you hand anyone money. Many now operate through apps (Bamboo, i-invest, Meristem, and others), so “having a broker” increasingly just means having an account on a licensed platform.

What the CSCS is

The Central Securities Clearing System (CSCS) is the electronic vault where Nigerian shares live. There are no paper certificates anymore: when you buy, the CSCS records you as the owner under your CSCS account number and Clearing House Number (CHN). Dividends and corporate-action notices follow these records — which is why getting your details right matters.

Opening an account: what you need

  • BVN (Bank Verification Number) — links your identity across the financial system.
  • A valid ID — NIN slip, voter’s card, driver’s licence or international passport.
  • A bank account — for funding purchases and receiving dividends and sale proceeds.
  • A passport photo and a signature, usually uploaded digitally.

First-timers can also enter the market through public offers — the Dangote Refinery IPO, for example, allots shares via a temporary Registrar ID even if you have no CSCS account yet (see our IPO guide).

Quick summary

  • A licensed stockbroker (or licensed investing app) executes your buy and sell orders.
  • Your CSCS account is where your shares are recorded electronically — guard its details.
  • BVN + valid ID + bank account is all it takes to open one; only use SEC-licensed firms.
